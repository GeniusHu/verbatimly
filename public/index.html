How to transcribe video to text in three steps

  1. Drop a file. Your browser reads the video and extracts the audio track. The video itself never leaves your computer, which is why a 400 MB file is ready in seconds — it was never uploaded.
  2. We transcribe the audio. The compressed audio track goes to our server, runs through a speech model, and comes back as timestamped segments. A 10-minute file is usually done before you switch tabs.
  3. You edit and export. Fix a name, label the speakers, then take the transcript as TXT, SRT or VTT. Nothing is kept on our side once the request finishes.

What people transcribe

  • Videos → text. Interviews, lectures, webinars, screen recordings — turn any video into a searchable transcript. Upload it to YouTube as captions and the video starts ranking for phrases people actually said in it.
  • Podcasts → text. Publish episodes as blog posts, or pull quotes without scrubbing through audio. An hour-long episode comes back with timestamps you can cite and link to.
  • Meetings → text. Record the call, drop the file, keep decisions and action items in writing. Nobody has to take notes, and nobody argues later about what was agreed.

Why the file is processed on your device

Most online converters upload the whole video to a server, which means waiting for a 500 MB file to travel twice and trusting someone else's storage with it. Verbatimly only needs the audio track, and the audio track can be made locally. That single decision is why the upload step takes a second instead of ten minutes, why nothing has to be cleaned up afterwards, and why the service can stay free for occasional use.

SRT or VTT? Which one you need

SRT

The safe default. Every video platform, editor and player reads it: YouTube, Premiere, Final Cut, DaVinci, VLC. If you are uploading captions somewhere, start here.

VTT

What the web uses. HTML5 video players take VTT natively, and it supports styling and positioning that SRT does not. Pick VTT if the video lives on your own site.

Both are plain text with the same timestamps. Convert between them by renaming the file and swapping commas for periods — or export both here, they are both free.

Putting the transcript on YouTube

  1. Export SRT from the transcript toolbar.
  2. Open YouTube Studio, go to Subtitles, pick the video and choose Add language.
  3. Select "Upload file" then "With timing", and choose the .srt you downloaded.
  4. Check the first and last line in the editor. If the sync drifts, re-upload rather than nudging line by line.

YouTube accepts SRT, VTT and SBV. Uploaded captions are indexed by search, so the video starts showing up for phrases people actually said in it.

Questions people actually ask.

Is it really free?

Yes. Three files a day, up to 30 minutes each, no account required. The transcript, every export and speaker labelling are free. A credit pack is the only paid product: it adds 5 more files that do not expire and raises the limit to 60 minutes per file. There is no subscription and no batch upload.

Do I need to sign up?

No email, no account, no card. Drop a file and take the transcript.

Which formats work?

MP4, MOV, MKV, WebM and AVI for video; MP3, M4A, WAV, FLAC, OGG and AAC for audio. If a MOV fails to decode, export it as MP4 first.

How long can a file be?

Free: up to 30 minutes and 500 MB per file. With a credit pack: up to 60 minutes and 500 MB per file.

How fast is it?

Transcription runs on our servers, so there is no AI model to download and nothing to install. A 10-minute video is usually done in under a minute.

Can it tell who is speaking?

You can label speakers yourself: click the S tag on any line to cycle Speaker 1, 2 or 3. The labels carry into SRT and VTT exports. Automatic detection is not offered.

Can I export subtitles?

Yes — TXT, SRT and VTT files are all free, ready to upload to YouTube or any video editor.

What happens to my files?

We do not store your uploads. The audio is processed and discarded, and the transcript is returned to your browser only. Nothing is used to train models or reviewed by a person.
